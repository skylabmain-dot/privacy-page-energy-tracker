Privacy Policy

Helio (Energy tracker) for iPhone, Apple Watch and Mac · Effective 2 August 2026

This policy describes what Helio stores, where it goes, and what it does not do. It is written from the app's actual behaviour rather than from a template, and it is meant to be read in full — it is short on purpose.

The short version.

  • Helio needs no account. On first launch it creates an anonymous account so your data has somewhere to live; you are never asked for a name, an email or anything else.
  • Your habits, your daily logs and your notes are stored under that account on Google's Firebase, and cached on your device.
  • There are no ads, no advertising identifier, no third-party tracking or attribution SDKs, and nothing is ever sold or shared for marketing.
  • You can delete everything from inside the app, permanently.

1. Who is responsible

Helio is developed and operated by an individual developer, Oleksandr Slobodianiuk. For any question about this policy, or to exercise any of the rights described below, write to alexslkmain@gmail.com.

2. Your account and identity

Helio uses Firebase Authentication (Google).

  • By default you are anonymous. On first launch the app signs you in anonymously. That produces a random user identifier (a UID) which exists only so that your data can be filed somewhere. It is not linked to your name, your email, your phone number or your Apple ID.
  • Sign-in is optional and exists for one reason: to make the same data available on more than one device. If you choose Sign in with Apple or Sign in with Google, the provider passes an account identifier and, depending on what you allow, an email address and a display name to Firebase Authentication. If you use Apple's "Hide My Email", the app only ever sees the relay address Apple generates. Helio never sees or stores your password.
  • The Mac companion requires sign-in, because it talks to the server directly and an anonymous session there would create a second, empty account.

3. What Helio stores about your use

Everything below lives under users/{your uid} in Google Cloud Firestore, readable and writable only by that account:

  • habits — the habits you create: title, optional description, whether it is an Inspire or an Exhaust, its energy value, icon and colour, schedule, and reminder settings.
  • dailyLogs — one document per calendar day, holding what you logged that day and the day's score.
  • notes — the free text you write on a habit for a given day.
  • habitVotes — your "useful" / "not for me" votes on the suggested-habit catalogue.
  • meta — your app settings (appearance, language, day structure, energy limits, display toggles) and your bookmarked suggestions.
  • aiChatMessages, aiInsights, aiUsage — reserved for the AI assistant described in section 10. That feature is switched off in this version, so nothing is written to them. They can only ever be written by our server, never by the app on your device.

When you vote on a suggested habit, the app also increments a shared, app-wide counter of likes, dislikes and additions for that suggestion. That counter is a number only — it carries no user identifier and nothing about you can be read back out of it.

Copies kept on your device

  • An offline cache of the data above, so the app works without a connection.
  • A small local copy of a few settings (appearance, language, whether the intro has been seen, and whether you have agreed to the "Discuss with AI" feature) so the app can start correctly before it reaches the network.
  • A shared container that the home screen widgets, the Apple Watch app and the Mac app read: today's ring, your habit list, and taps that have not yet reached the phone. It is a display cache and a queue, never the record of truth.

Deleting the app removes all of this from the device.

4. Analytics

The iPhone app includes Firebase Analytics. The companions — widgets, Apple Watch, Mac — contain no analytics at all.

The app itself sends exactly five events, all of them about the first-launch intro, and none of them containing anything you typed:

  • onboarding_page_viewed — page number and page name
  • onboarding_skipped — page number
  • onboarding_completed — how it ended (finished, skipped, or via sign-in)
  • onboarding_sign_in_tapped — page number
  • onboarding_sign_in_succeeded

Each also carries whether the intro was the real first launch or a replay from Settings. Every parameter is a whole number or a short fixed keyword. No habit names, no notes, no free text, no personal data ever leaves the app through analytics.

Separately from those events, the Firebase Analytics SDK collects a standard set of automatic events and device properties defined by Google — for example installs, session starts, app version, device model and coarse region. That set is Google's, not ours; we neither extend nor configure it. See Firebase's own privacy documentation for what it contains.

Helio does not use the advertising identifier (IDFA), shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt, and declares no tracking in its App Store privacy manifest.

5. Crash reports and error diagnostics

The app links Firebase Crashlytics, and crash collection is enabled at launch. When the app crashes or hits a handled error, Crashlytics records the technical detail: the stack trace, the app version, the device model and OS version, and a small set of keys we attach so a report can be located — the screen or operation involved, your user identifier, and, where relevant, the internal identifier of a habit or a date key such as 2026-08-02. Habit titles and note text are never attached.

6. The developer's error channel

Because Helio is run by one person, non-fatal errors are also mirrored to a private Telegram channel that only the developer reads, so problems are noticed the day they happen. What is sent there:

  • Errors: the system error description, the operation it came from, and the same identifiers as above (user identifier, habit identifier, date key), plus a timestamp, the app version, the iOS version and the device model.
  • Crash notices: a line on the next launch saying the previous session crashed, with app version, iOS version and device model.
  • Account events: that a sign-in, sign-out or account deletion happened, with the provider name and the user identifier.
  • Feedback you send: if you use Settings → Send Feedback, the message you wrote is delivered here, together with your user identifier, app version, iOS version and device model. This channel is how your message reaches a human.

Habit titles, note text and daily logs are never sent to this channel.

7. Widgets, Apple Watch and Mac

The home screen widgets and the Apple Watch app never talk to a server. They read the shared on-device container described in section 3, and any tap you make there is handed back to the iPhone app, which records it. The Watch receives its copy from your iPhone directly, over Apple's device-to-device connection.

The Mac companion is a full client and reads and writes the same Firestore data as the iPhone, under the account you signed in with.

8. Notifications

Habit reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device by iOS. There is no push service, no device token, and nothing about your reminders is sent anywhere.

9. "Discuss with AI" in Settings

Settings offers two rows that hand a summary of your habit data to Claude or to ChatGPT so you can talk it through with an assistant you already use. This is entirely opt-in, and it works like this:

  • The first time you use each service, a dialog explains what is about to be shared and asks you to confirm. Nothing is prepared or opened until you do. The confirmation is remembered on that device only.
  • The summary is built on your device from data already in the app: how many habits you have, how many days you have logged, your average scores and recent trend, the last few days with the titles of what you completed, per-habit completion rates and streaks, and totals by time of day and weekday. Your notes are not included.
  • The summary is copied to your clipboard, and the app then opens claude.ai or chatgpt.com in your browser with the text prefilled. Nothing is submitted for you — you review it and press send.

Helio sends nothing to Anthropic or OpenAI itself. The conversation happens in your own account with that service, and what happens to the text afterwards is governed by your agreement with them.

10. The AI assistant tab — not active in this version

The app binary contains an AI assistant feature: a tab with insight cards about your own history and a chat that can answer questions about it. It is disabled by a server-side switch, and both of its halves default to off. While the switch is off the tab is not merely locked — it is absent, there is no way to reach it, and the server refuses every request regardless of what the app asks. In this version, no habit data is sent to Anthropic or to any AI provider by the app.

It is described here because the code paths are present and we would rather state plainly what they would do. If the feature is ever switched on, it would work like this: your request and the relevant slice of your own data — habits, logged days, and notes on the days in question — would be sent from our Cloud Function to Anthropic's API to produce an answer. It would be read-only: the assistant can look at your data but has no ability to log, change or delete anything. This policy and the App Store privacy disclosure will be updated before that switch is turned on, not after.

11. Update check

To tell you when a newer version is available, the app occasionally asks Apple's public App Store lookup service about its own bundle identifier. Nothing about you is included in that request.

12. What Helio does not do

  • No advertising, no ad networks, no sponsored content.
  • No sale of data, and no sharing of data for anyone else's marketing.
  • No third-party tracking, attribution or profiling SDKs. The only external software in the app is Google's Firebase and Google Sign-In.
  • No location, no HealthKit or health data, no contacts, no photos, no microphone, no camera. The app requests none of these permissions.
  • No marketing email. Your address, if the app ever receives one, is used for sign-in and for replying to you.
  • No purchases or subscriptions in this version.

13. Who processes your data

  • Google — Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Analytics, Crashlytics, App Check and Cloud Functions host and process the data described above. See the Firebase privacy documentation and the Google Privacy Policy. If you use Google Sign-In, Google also acts as your identity provider.
  • Apple — if you use Sign in with Apple. See the Apple Privacy Policy.
  • Telegram — carries the developer error and feedback channel described in section 6. See the Telegram Privacy Policy.
  • Anthropic and OpenAI — only if you use "Discuss with AI" (section 9), and then in your own account with them, not ours.

These providers operate globally, so your data may be stored and processed outside your country, including in the United States. Traffic between the app and these services is encrypted in transit.

14. Deleting your data

In the app: Settings → Account → Delete Account. If you are signed in, iOS asks you to authenticate once more to confirm it is you.

This deletes your habits, daily logs, notes, votes, bookmarks and settings, the AI documents in section 3 if any exist, and then the sign-in account itself. It is permanent and there is no backup to restore from. The app then starts again as a new anonymous user.

Two things it does not reach, because they are stored separately by Google and not filed under your habit data: analytics records and crash reports. If you want those removed as well, email us and we will delete what those tools allow. Uninstalling the app removes every local copy from that device.

Data you have not deleted is kept for as long as the account exists, because it is the app's content — a habit log from two years ago is the point of the app. Error and feedback messages in the developer channel are kept only as long as they are useful for fixing the problem.

15. Your rights

Depending on where you live — in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, Ukraine, California and elsewhere — you have the right to ask what personal data is held about you, to obtain a copy, to have it corrected, to have it deleted, to object to or restrict its processing, and to withdraw consent you previously gave. Deletion is available immediately in the app; for anything else, write to alexslkmain@gmail.com and it will be handled. You also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

The legal basis for processing is the performance of the service you asked for (storing and syncing your habits), your consent where the app asks for it explicitly (notifications, "Discuss with AI"), and a legitimate interest in keeping the app working (crash and error diagnostics).

16. Children

Helio is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13, or under the higher age of consent that applies in some countries. If you believe a child has provided personal data through the app, write to us and it will be deleted.
